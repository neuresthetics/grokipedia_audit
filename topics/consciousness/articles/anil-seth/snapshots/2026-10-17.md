**C0001** Anil Seth is a British neuroscientist specializing in cognitive and computational neuroscience, particularly the scientific study of consciousness, and serves as Professor of Cognitive and Computational Neuroscience at the University of Sussex, where he also directs the Sussex Centre for Consciousness Science. [1] [2]

**C0002** Born in 1972 in Oxford, England, Seth earned a B.A. in Natural Sciences from the University of Cambridge in 1994, followed by an M.Sc. in Knowledge-Based Systems from the University of Sussex in 1996, and a D.Phil. in Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence from the same institution in 2001. [3] **C0003** After completing his doctorate, he held a postdoctoral fellowship and associate fellowship at The Neurosciences Institute in San Diego from 2001 to 2006, before joining the University of Sussex as permanent faculty in 2006. [1] [3] **C0004** He advanced to Reader (Associate Professor) in the School of Informatics and became Director of the Sussex Centre for Consciousness Science in 2010, later assuming his current professorship in 2012. [1] [3]

**C0005** Seth's research focuses on the brain mechanisms underlying consciousness, perception, and selfhood, employing predictive processing frameworks and neuroimaging techniques to explore how the brain generates subjective experience. [1] [2] **C0006** He co-directs the CIFAR Program on Brain, Mind, and Consciousness and leads the Dreamachine project, an initiative using stroboscopic light to induce shared visual experiences for consciousness research. [1] [2] **C0007** As Founding Editor-in-Chief of the journal Neuroscience of Consciousness from 2014 to 2024, he has shaped the field's discourse. [2]

**C0008** Among his notable contributions, Seth authored the bestselling book Being You: A New Science of Consciousness (2021), which synthesizes decades of research on the predictive nature of perception and consciousness, and co-edited 30-Second Brain (2014), a popular science overview of neuroscience topics. ### Consciousness science

**C0044** Anil Seth has developed a influential perspective on consciousness, framing it as a form of controlled hallucination in which the brain generates subjective experiences by predicting and constructing sensory realities rather than merely receiving passive inputs. **C0045** This theory posits that perceptions are actively shaped by the brain's internal models, constrained by sensory evidence to align with the external world, thereby enabling adaptive behavior and self-awareness. **C0046** Seth articulates this view in detail through his synthesis of neuroscientific evidence, emphasizing how such generative processes underpin the vividness and personal nature of conscious experience.

**C0047** To empirically probe these ideas, Seth's research employs advanced neuroimaging techniques, including functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and electroencephalography (EEG), to examine disorders of consciousness such as vegetative states and anesthesia-induced unconsciousness. **C0048** These methods allow detection of residual awareness through patterns of brain activity, like task-based decoding of imagined movements or perturbations via transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), revealing that up to 15-20% of clinically diagnosed unresponsive patients may retain covert consciousness. **C0049** Such experiments not only refine diagnostic tools but also illuminate the neural boundaries of conscious states, showing how disruptions in predictive mechanisms contribute to loss of awareness. [26]

**C0050** Seth's contributions extend to integrating first-person phenomenological descriptions—such as subjective reports of qualia and presence—with third-person neuroscience data, fostering a naturalistic approach to the hard problem of consciousness. **C0051** Rather than treating subjective experience as an inexplicable add-on to physical processes, he advocates building explanatory bridges that account for why specific brain dynamics give rise to felt experiences, drawing on embodied and predictive frameworks to demystify qualia without resorting to dualism. **C0052** This integrative method prioritizes testable hypotheses over philosophical standoffs, advancing a science of consciousness grounded in biological realism.

**C0053** Central to Seth's framework are concepts like phenomenological control, the capacity of the brain to modulate conscious content in accordance with prior expectancies and beliefs, as evidenced in studies of hypnotic suggestion and perceptual illusions where participants generate experiences on demand. **C0054** Complementing this, he emphasizes interoception's role in selfhood, arguing that the minimal sense of self arises from predictive inferences about internal bodily signals, such as heartbeats and visceral sensations, which anchor the "I" in physiological homeostasis and emotional valence. **C0055** These ideas highlight how consciousness is deeply embodied, emerging from the brain's ongoing regulation of the living body.

**C0056** By 2025, Seth has authored over 340 peer-reviewed publications, including seminal works on the emergence of consciousness in complex neural systems through measures of integration and differentiation, which underscore how global brain states differentiate conscious from unconscious processing. **C0057** A milestone in this trajectory is his 2025 co-authored article in Frontiers in Science, which provides an overview of recent developments in consciousness science and proposes future interdisciplinary paradigms for advancing the field, such as combined EEG/fMRI protocols and computational modeling. **C0058** This body of work positions consciousness as an adaptive, brain-bound phenomenon, informed briefly by predictive processing as a computational substrate for hallucination-like generation. ### Predictive processing and related theories

**C0059** Anil Seth has advanced the predictive processing framework, positing the brain as a hierarchical prediction machine that generates top-down expectations about sensory inputs to minimize prediction errors, thereby enabling efficient perception and action. **C0060** In this view, perception arises not as passive reception of sensory data but as active inference, where the brain continuously updates its generative models to reduce discrepancies between predicted and actual sensory signals. **C0061** Seth's adaptations emphasize sensorimotor contingencies, integrating predictions with bodily actions to explain perceptual presence, such as why certain stimuli feel vividly "present" while others do not. **C0062** This approach draws on Bayesian principles, where perceptions are probabilistic inferences optimizing for accuracy and simplicity. [29] [30]

**C0063** A cornerstone of Seth's modeling involves Granger causality to dissect directed interactions in neural dynamics, providing a statistical tool to infer causal influences from time-series data in brain networks. **C0064** By applying Granger causality, Seth and collaborators have characterized how neural populations exhibit emergent connectivity patterns during behavior, distinguishing effective connectivity from mere correlations. **C0065** This method, extended through graph-theoretic analyses, reveals causal cores—subsets of interactions critical for behavioral outputs—in simulated and empirical neural systems. **C0066** For instance, frequency-domain decompositions of conditional Granger causality allow mapping of dynamic causal flows, aiding the study of complexity in brain-wide networks. [31] [32] [33]

**C0067** Seth's theoretical contributions incorporate Bayesian inference equations tailored to perceptual processes, often framed within the free-energy principle. **C0068** Central to this is the minimization of surprise, quantified as the self-information or negative log-probability of sensory inputs:

**C0070** where $ S $ represents surprise, $ P(x) $ is the probability of sensory data $ x $ under the brain's model, and the system seeks to bound this via variational free energy $ F $, an upper bound on surprise that approximates Bayesian posteriors through gradient descent. **C0071** In Seth's applications, this formulation underpins active inference, where actions are selected to fulfill predictions and resolve uncertainties, as seen in models of sensorimotor learning. **C0072** These derivations highlight how hierarchical predictive coding resolves perceptual ambiguities by propagating errors bidirectionally across neural layers. [30] [34]

**C0073** Seth extends predictive processing to artificial intelligence, arguing in a 2025 target article that conscious AI requires more than computational equivalence to biological systems, advocating biological naturalism to emphasize embodied, homeostatic processes over disembodied algorithms. **C0074** This challenges substrate-independent views of consciousness, positing that AI's lack of biological grounding limits its capacity for genuine subjective experience, while urging integration of predictive principles into AI architectures for safer, more interpretable systems. **C0075** His analyses of causality and emergence in brain networks further inform these extensions, modeling complexity as arising from adaptive inference in open, self-organizing systems. **C0076** Empirically, Seth's Dreamachine project investigates induced visual hallucinations through stroboscopic flicker, using predictive processing to interpret how sensory perturbations disrupt error-minimizing predictions, yielding insights into hallucinatory mechanisms. **C0077** Complementing this, the ERC-funded CONSCIOUS project (2021–2026) employs advanced neuroimaging and computational modeling to mechanistically unpack conscious experiences, testing predictive frameworks against neural data from altered states. **C0078** These initiatives operationalize theoretical predictions, linking abstract models to measurable brain dynamics.
